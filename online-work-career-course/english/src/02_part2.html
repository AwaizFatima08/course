4.3 How to choose your first two platforms

  1. Where do your clients buy? Designers and developers often find work on Upwork and Fiverr; writers and VAs also use Upwork; UK-based work often appears on PeoplePerHour; European tech consultants may prefer Malt.
  2. How much competition? Popular categories on big marketplaces have many bidders. Niche categories may have less competition but fewer jobs.
  3. Can you survive the first 10 jobs? Choose a platform that lets you start without paying a large upfront membership.
  4. Pick one "bidding" platform (where you send proposals) and one "selling" platform (where clients buy a ready package).
Recommended startFor most beginners: Upwork (bidding and long projects) plus Fiverr (packaged services). Add a portfolio site (Behance, Dribbble or GitHub) and a LinkedIn profile in the first month.

4.5 Summary

  • Platforms fall into marketplaces, vetted networks, portfolio sites, professional networks and job boards.
  • Read each platform's fee page; headline rates and effective rates differ.
  • Start with one bidding and one selling platform, and build your own presence alongside them.

Chapter 5

Upwork: Account, Profile and Bidding

Step-by-step account creation, a strong freelancer profile, skill tests, Connects and how to win your first contract.

Learning goals. You will create and verify an Upwork freelancer account, set up a profile that gets read, and send your first well-targeted proposals.

5.1 What Upwork is

Upwork is one of the largest freelance marketplaces. Clients post jobs (hourly or fixed-price) and freelancers submit proposals. It covers writing, design, development, admin, marketing, customer support and many other categories. It suits people who like to discuss a project and agree on its scope before starting.

5.2 Creating your account (step by step)

Figure 5.1 · The Upwork onboarding sequence
  1. Go to upwork.com and select "Sign up". Use a professional email address (for example, first name and surname). Choose the option to sign up as a freelancer, not as a client. Keep your password in a password manager.
  2. Enter your basic details. Name as it appears on your government ID, country, primary language and the type of work you do. Use your real name; mismatched names can delay verification.
  3. Choose your categories and specialisation. Pick one main category now (for example "Writing" or "Graphic Design"). You can add others later.
  4. Set up identity verification. Upload a government ID and complete any selfie or phone check. Verification can take a few days.
  5. Add a payment method. Upwork lists the withdrawal methods available in your country (bank transfer, PayPal, Payoneer and others, depending on region). Confirm the fees and currency before choosing.
  6. Build your profile (section 5.3) before you bid on anything.
  7. Take the skill tests that match your categories (section 5.5). A passed test is shown on your profile.
  8. Buy or use Connects (section 5.4) and send your first proposals.
Check on the day you sign upUpwork's sign-up screens and onboarding steps change from time to time. If a step looks different, follow what the screen asks and read Upwork's help centre articles on "freelancer profile" and "verification". Never pay anyone who claims they can "guarantee" your account approval.

5.3 Writing a profile that gets read

Clients look at four things in order: your title, your first lines of overview, your hourly rate and your portfolio. Build them in this order.

  • Title (one line): Name your service and the result you give. Good: "Proofreader and Copy Editor for Business English". Weak: "Hardworking writer".
  • Overview (first 3 lines count most): Say who you help, what you deliver and one proof point. Then describe your process in short paragraphs or bullets, and close with a call to action.
  • Skills (up to 15): Use the exact words clients type in search, such as "SEO writing", "WordPress" or "Canva". Remove skills you cannot show evidence for.
  • Hourly rate: Start realistically (see Chapter 14). You can raise it after your first reviews.
  • Portfolio: Add 3–5 items with images, a description and the result. Chapter 9 gives a case-study template.
  • Work history and education: Include relevant past jobs, volunteer work and courses, even if they were not paid.
  • Photo: A clear, friendly head-and-shoulders photo in good light. No group photos, filters or logos.

5.4 Understanding Connects

On Upwork, each proposal costs a small number of Connects. Connects are spent when you submit a proposal and are generally not refunded if the client does not reply. Plans include different monthly allowances, and extra Connects can be bought. Check the current cost of Connects per proposal and the plan allowances on upwork.com, since these have changed several times.

Spend wiselyDo not spend Connects on jobs you cannot deliver within the budget and timeline. Ten targeted proposals are worth more than fifty generic ones.

5.5 Skill tests and badges

Upwork offers skill tests (multiple-choice or short practical tests) and an English assessment for many categories. A passed test appears as a badge on your profile. Take only tests that match your strengths; a low score can be visible to clients. Use the test preparation guides in Upwork's help centre and practise the topic before you start.

5.6 Finding and applying to jobs on Upwork

  1. Use the search bar with specific words (for example "product description", "Shopify", "blog post SEO") and set filters for Fixed-price or Hourly, Entry level and Payment verified.
  2. Open the job and read the full description. Check the budget, duration, client history (hire rate, total spent, reviews) and the number of proposals.
  3. Save promising jobs. Apply to 5–10 per day at most, so each proposal can be personal.
  4. Write your proposal (Chapter 12), attach a relevant sample, and submit.
  5. Check your Messages every day. Reply within a few hours where possible.

5.7 After you win a job: contracts, milestones and reviews

  • Fixed-price contracts can use milestones; the client funds each milestone before you start it, which protects both sides.
  • Hourly contracts track time with Upwork's tracker. Use it honestly; screenshots are required in some cases.
  • Keep all communication on the platform. Moving clients off-platform in the early stage can break Upwork's terms and removes your protection.
  • When the job is finished, ask politely for a review. Reviews carry more weight than anything in your profile.

5.8 Summary

  • Sign up as a freelancer, verify your identity and payment method, then build the profile before bidding.
  • Write a clear title and the first lines of your overview; these decide whether clients read further.
  • Spend Connects on targeted proposals and keep all work and messages on the platform.

Fiverr: Gigs, Packages and Levels

How to create a Fiverr seller account, design a gig that buyers choose, and climb the seller levels.

Learning goals. You will create a seller account, publish a gig with three packages, and understand the seller levels and how buyers search.

6.1 What Fiverr is

Fiverr is a marketplace where sellers publish fixed services called gigs, and buyers choose from listings. Your gig is like a shop window: buyers see the image, title, starting price and reviews before they message you. It suits people who can describe a service clearly and deliver it in a repeatable way.

6.2 Creating a seller account

  1. Go to fiverr.com and sign up with email, Google or another login option. Choose a username that is short and professional.
  2. Switch to selling via the "Become a Seller" option. Complete the seller onboarding: your name, country, language, skills, and phone verification.
  3. Set up your seller profile: a clear photo, a short description, your languages and education or certificates. Keep the tone friendly and specific.
  4. Add a payout method (bank or other supported options in your country) and review Fiverr's current seller fee on its official help pages. Public sources commonly report a 20% seller commission on earnings, so confirm this before pricing your gigs.
  5. Create your first gig (section 6.3). Publish only when you can deliver the lowest package well.

6.4 Designing your three packages

Use the same wording style across packages so buyers can compare them quickly. Show clearly what changes between them: quantity, speed, revisions and extras. Prices above are examples only; set yours after researching similar gigs.

6.5 Writing the title, description and FAQ

  • Title: "I will [do what] for [who] [result]". Example: "I will write SEO product descriptions for your online shop". Keep it under the platform's character limit and include your main keyword once.
  • Description: Explain the service, who it is for, what the buyer must send you, and what they receive. Use short paragraphs and bullet points.
  • Requirements: Ask only for what you need (product list, brand guidelines, photos). Good requirements prevent delays.
  • FAQ: Answer the three questions buyers ask most, such as file formats, revision policy and usage rights.

6.6 Seller levels

Fiverr describes seller levels such as New Seller, Level 1, Level 2 and Top Rated. Levels are based on criteria like completed orders, ratings, response time and on-time delivery. Higher levels improve visibility and trust, but each level must be earned again if performance drops. Check the current criteria in Fiverr's seller help pages, since they change.

Fiverr ProFiverr Pro is a separate, invitation or application-based tier for professional sellers who pass a vetting process, usually after building a strong track record and portfolio. Do not expect it in your first months.

6.7 Getting buyers to choose you

  • Reply to every buyer message within a few hours.
  • Deliver early when you can, and always deliver a little more than the package promises (for example, an extra sample or a checklist).
  • Ask for a review after delivery, politely and without pressure.
  • Use "Promote" and offers carefully; test a small budget and watch the results before spending more.
  • Create 2–3 related gigs rather than one vague gig, for example "Product description", "Blog article" and "Email newsletter".

6.8 Summary

  • Fiverr sells fixed, packaged gigs; buyers decide from the image, title, price and reviews.
  • Use three clear packages, a keyword-rich title and a gallery that proves your work.
  • Check Fiverr's official fee and level criteria, and respond quickly to build your rating.
